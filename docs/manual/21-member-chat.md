# 21. Member chat

> **Written for readers with no coding experience.** This manual assumes no programming or
> technical knowledge of any kind — only that you use the Badhan app.

[← Previous: Donor feedback and new donor registration](20-donor-feedback.md) · [Contents](README.md)

---

## What it is

One conversation, shared by **every member of Badhan across every hall**. Not a message to one
person, not a group per hall — one room that everybody at Volunteer level or above is in.

Use it for the things that used to go round on the phone: a request that needs a donor tonight,
a reminder about tomorrow's camp, a call for volunteers on Friday.

**Donors cannot see it and cannot write to it.** Neither can anyone who has been taken back down
to donor: the moment somebody stops being a member, the chat stops working for them, and the app
will ask them to sign in again.

## The two ways in

**The round button in the top-right corner.** It is on every screen once you are signed in, and it
does not move when you scroll. Tap it and the conversation opens over whatever you were doing — on
a phone it slides up from the bottom, on a computer it opens as a panel beside the button. Tap it
again, or the ✕, to close it.

**Messages, in the menu.** The same conversation as a full page, with room to read. Everything is
the same; there is just more of it on screen. The round button hides itself while you are on that
page, because it would only open the page you are already looking at.

Use whichever suits you. They are the same room, and a message sent from one appears in the other.

## The most important thing on this page

**Messages do not arrive on their own.**

The app only checks for new messages when:

- you open the app,
- you press **Fetch messages**, or
- you send something.

That is the whole list. If you leave the app open on the table for an hour, **nothing new will
appear** until you ask for it. The conversation on your screen is what the app was told about the
last time it asked, not what is there right now.

This is not a fault and it is not a bug. It is how the feature is built, and it is why the
**Fetch messages** button sits at the top of the conversation where you cannot miss it. Press it
whenever you want to know if anything has come in.

## The red number on the button

The round button shows a red number when there are messages you have not looked at.

**It counts messages the app has already fetched.** It is not a count of everything waiting on the
server — the app has no way to know that without asking, and it only asks when you tell it to. So
read the number as *"something came in since you last opened the chat"* rather than as an exact
tally of everything you have missed.

Two things about it are worth knowing:

- **Your own messages never count.** You do not have unread mail from yourself.
- **It clears when you open the chat, and only then.** If a message arrives while you already have
  the chat open, the number goes up behind it and stays up. Close the chat and open it again to
  clear it.

If the number ever reads **99+**, it means more than ninety-nine — the button stops counting there.

## Coming back after a few days

**One press of Fetch messages brings back thirty messages at most.**

If you have been away and more than thirty have piled up, the button changes to say **More
messages waiting**. Press it again. Keep pressing until it goes back to saying **Fetch messages** —
that is how you know you have everything.

**Nothing is lost in between.** You are being handed the backlog a page at a time, oldest first,
so the conversation stays in order. It is deliberately not done in one go: on a slow connection,
fetching several hundred messages at once is how an app appears to freeze.

## Reading older messages

Scroll up. When you reach the top, the app fetches the previous page and keeps your place — the
message you were reading stays where it was rather than jumping.

Keep going and you will eventually see **This is the start of the chat**. There is nothing older;
that is the beginning of the room.

## The chat needs a connection, every time

Nothing about the chat is kept on your phone.

Donor searches you have already done can still be looked at with no internet. **The chat cannot.**
Open the app with no connection and the conversation is **empty** — not because messages were lost,
and not because anything has gone wrong. They come back the moment you are online and the app
fetches again.

If the chat looks empty and you were expecting messages, check your connection first.

## Writing a message

Type in the box at the bottom and press the send arrow. A message can be up to **2000 characters**;
the counter under the box tells you how many you have used. An empty message, or one that is only
spaces, will not send.

On a computer, **Enter** sends and **Shift+Enter** starts a new line. On a phone, **Enter** always
starts a new line and only the arrow sends — otherwise half of a two-line message would go out
before you finished writing it.

The box grows as you add lines, but only up to about five lines — after that it scrolls inside
itself instead of getting taller, and the conversation above it shrinks to make room. The line you
are typing and the send arrow therefore stay on screen no matter how long the message gets.

If a message fails to send, **what you typed stays in the box**. Fix whatever the app complains
about and press send again. You never have to retype it.

There is a limit of **twenty messages a minute**. It is far more than an ordinary conversation
needs, and it is there so that nothing can flood a room every member reads.

## Who wrote what

Each message shows the sender's **name, batch, hall and role** — for example
*Mir Mahathir · 16 · Titumir · Hall Admin*. Your own messages sit on the right; everyone else's on
the left.

Those details are read fresh every time, from the person's record as it is **now**. So somebody
promoted last week shows their new role on messages they sent last month. It also means a message
can be labelled **Donor** if that person has since been taken back down to donor — the message is
still theirs, and the label is simply their role today.

If a donor record has been deleted altogether, their messages remain and are labelled **Former
member**. Deleting an account does not delete other people's conversation.

## Deleting a message

**Your own, at any time.** Tap the small bin icon on your message and confirm.

**A super admin can delete anybody's.** Nobody else can — not even a hall admin, because this room
is not divided by hall.

**Nothing can be edited.** There is no way to change a message after it is sent. If you got
something wrong, delete it and send another.

**A deleted message simply disappears.** No "message deleted" marker is left in its place. Anyone
who fetches the conversation afterwards sees no trace of it at all — so unless they happened to be
looking at the time, they will never know it was there. Somebody who already had it on screen keeps
seeing it until their next fetch.

Deleting is permanent for everyone in the room. A super admin can still find what was removed in
the activity log ([chapter 15](15-statistics-and-reports.md)), which is the only record that
survives.

## Things worth being clear about

**Nothing here is private.** Every message goes to every member in every hall. There are no
private messages, no smaller groups, and no way to send something to one person. Write as though
the whole of Badhan is reading it, because it is.

**Nothing is ever deleted automatically.** Messages do not expire and are not cleared out with age.
What is written stays until somebody deletes it by hand.

**There are no notifications.** The app will not alert you when a message arrives, and closing it
means you will hear nothing until you open it again. If something is genuinely urgent, phone the
person.

## In guest mode

The guest login shows an invented conversation so you can see how the feature works. The messages
are made up, anything you send goes nowhere, and **no new message ever arrives** — so **Fetch
messages** correctly reports nothing new every time you press it. That is the demonstration
behaving properly, not the button failing.
